01 / The layer

Definition

A layer, not a chat window.

When agents plan, execute and delegate on their own, there has to be a level above them. Not another window to type into. A layer that records who owns the task, what the agent may do, when it must stop and which evidence exists.1

  1. 01
    ObservableYou see what the agent does and why.
  2. 02
    BoundedTasks have scope, permissions and stop rules.
  3. 03
    VerifiableResults need evidence, not plausibility.
  4. 04
    Human-ownedDecisions stay with a person.

Boundary. HAI is not a chat interface and not a finished enterprise platform. HAI is an approach and a control layer for human-owned agentic work.

02 / The problem

Why HAI exists

The problem isn't the agent.

It is the gap between execution and accountability. Once several agents work together, nobody can say precisely who caused what, which limits applied, and why a result counts as done.

HAI closes that gap not with more automation, but with a layer that makes work observable, bounded, verifiable and human-owned.

Without HAI: the work is fast. The responsibility is invisible.

03 / Proof

Three parts

Three parts. One principle.

PenEcho — the agent writes back on the same page.

You work with a pen on a reMarkable. The agent reads the page, checks the step and writes a short hint back in ink — red for wrong, green for right. No chat window, no device switch. Your handwriting stays.
