The human as an agent

A person is more than an approval button. In human–agent work, both partners can propose, question and initiate a change; the human decides what to authorize and owns the direction.

A decision in practice

In a retrospective on an October 26 tablet tutor prototype, Samuel wanted a screen showing what the tutor saw, said and wrote. That view would have made the tutor's activity available for him to inspect and question. Work stopped after multiple agents redirected one shared session. Stopping was a decision about the conditions for continuing, not a finished implementation of the screen.

That distinction matters: the agents could offer approaches, but Samuel could decide whether to approve one, ask what it would reveal, or stop the work until the session was understandable again. Equal partnership means his judgment shapes the work throughout, while the authority to approve a consequential change remains explicit.

From an idea to a decision

An idea spoken aloud is a proposal until someone chooses it. Before treating it as a task, name the intended outcome, the boundary and who may decide. A person can change their mind or ask the agent to challenge the proposal; an agent can identify an ambiguity instead of quietly choosing on the person's behalf.

Completion also needs observable evidence. For a proposed screen, that could mean seeing what it actually displays and checking it against the intended view. A confident summary alone does not establish that the screen exists or works.
